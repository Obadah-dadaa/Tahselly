Every business owner asks the same question: **when should I remind the client?** Too early feels pushy; too late and the payment slips. The answer is a clear schedule your team knows and applies to every invoice the same way.

## The schedule we recommend

| Stage | When | Goal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before due | 3 days before | Get the payment into the client's run |
| Due date | Same day | Remind them today is the day |
| Overdue | After 7 days | Find out why it is late |
| Overdue | After 14 days | Clearer follow-up |
| Overdue | After 30 days | Ask for a specific payment date |
| Final notice | After 45 days | Last chance before escalation |

## Why three days before the due date?

Most companies prepare payment runs a few days before they execute them. If your reminder lands in that window, your invoice joins the next run. If it lands after the due date, it often waits for the following run, which can mean two extra weeks.

## Why follow up after 7 days, not after 1?

A day or two late is normal: a bank transfer in progress, or a weekend. Waiting a week gives the client reasonable time and makes your follow-up look professional rather than pushy.

## What about large clients and purchase orders?

Some large companies pay in fixed cycles (every 30 or 60 days, for example). In that case:

- Ask about their **payment cycle** at the start.
- Make sure your invoice carries the correct **PO number**.
- Time your first reminder before their cycle closes, not only before the due date.

## Don't remind by hand once you have 10+ invoices

With ten or more invoices, a manual schedule becomes exhausting: who do I remind today? Did I message them last week? That is where automation pays off. ## The bottom line

- Start **before** the due date, not after it.
- Follow up at clear intervals: 7, then 14, then 30 days.
- Give the final notice a specific deadline.
- Apply the same schedule to everyone. Consistency is what gets clients paying on time.
