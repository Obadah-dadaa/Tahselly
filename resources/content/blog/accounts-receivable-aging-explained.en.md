If you ask your accountant "how much do clients owe us?" and get a single number, that is not enough. **AED 200,000** due last week is very different from **AED 200,000** overdue for four months. That is what the **aging report** is for.

## What is an aging report?

It splits the money you have not yet collected by **how late it is**, meaning how many days have passed since the due date. The usual buckets:

| Bucket | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Not yet due | The invoice is not due yet |
| 1 – 30 days | Slightly late, usually easy to collect |
| 31 – 60 days | Needs serious follow-up |
| 61 – 90 days | Rising risk |
| Over 90 days | Much higher chance it won't be collected |

## How to read it in a minute

1. **Look at the last buckets first** (61–90 and 90+). That is the money at risk.
2. **Compare with last month.** If the 90+ bucket is growing, your follow-up has a problem.
3. **Look for repeat names.** Most of the overdue amount usually sits with a few clients.

## Two numbers to watch alongside it

- **Collection rate:** how much you collected versus how much you invoiced over a period. The closer to 100%, the better.
- **Days sales outstanding (DSO):** how many days it takes, on average, to collect an invoice. Lower is better.

## What to do after reading it

- **1–30 days:** regular automatic reminders are usually enough.
- **31–60 days:** a personal call; ask why it is late.
- **61–90 days:** agree on a specific payment date or an instalment plan.
- **Over 90 days:** a final notice, then legal advice if needed.
