An overdue invoice is rarely a bad client. Most of the time it was **forgotten**, lost in an accounts inbox, or waiting for a signature from a manager who is travelling. That is why the best way to collect is not pressure, but **steady follow-up** that keeps your invoice at the top of the pile.

## 1. Start before the invoice is late

The most common mistake is waiting for the due date, then calling two weeks later. Send a **friendly reminder three days before the due date** with the invoice number, the amount and how to pay. On its own, this noticeably reduces late payments, because the payment lands in the client's plan before they get busy.

## 2. Make paying as easy as possible

Every extra step delays payment. Make sure every message includes:

- **The invoice number and amount due**, clearly.
- **The due date**.
- **How to pay**: your IBAN or a payment link.
- **A named contact** in case there is a question.

## 3. Follow a fixed schedule, not your mood

When you follow up "when there is time", follow-up slips on busy days, which are exactly the days you need cash. Give every invoice a fixed schedule, for example:

| When | Message |
| --- | --- |
| 3 days before due | Friendly reminder |
| On the due date | The invoice is due today |
| 7 days after | Gentle follow-up |
| 14 and 30 days after | Clearer follow-up |
| 45 days after | Final notice |

## 4. Use the channel your client actually reads

In the UAE, many business owners read **WhatsApp** before email. Use email for the formal record and WhatsApp for a quick nudge. Combining the two raises response rates noticeably.

## 5. Keep the tone professional

- Don't accuse; assume good faith in the first message.
- Be direct about the amount and the date.
- Increase firmness with each follow-up, without threats.

> Tip: if the client pays part of the amount, record the partial payment right away and follow up on the remaining balance only. It shows you are organised and avoids awkwardness.

## 6. Know when to call and when to escalate

After 30 days with no reply, a phone call beats another message. Past 90 days, it is worth speaking to a lawyer or a collection firm about the options for your specific case.

## The bottom line

Successful collection is a **system**, not a heroic effort at the end of the month: a reminder before the due date, a fixed schedule, two channels and a professional tone. If you issue more than a handful of invoices a month, automating that schedule saves hours and gets clients paying on time without you chasing anyone yourself.
